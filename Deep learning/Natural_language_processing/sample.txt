The sun sets slowly behind the old mountain ridge, painting the sky in shades of orange and deep purple. Birds circle above the quiet valley, their wings catching the last golden light of the day.

In the little village below, children are still playing near the river, laughing as they skip stones across the water. Their voices carry far in the cool evening air.

An old storyteller sits on a wooden bench under the ancient oak tree. He has told tales here for more than sixty years. Tonight the children will gather again, bringing apples and warm blankets, waiting eagerly for another story.

"Once," the old man begins, his voice soft but strong, "there was a fox who could speak the language of the stars. Every night he climbed the highest hill and listened. The stars whispered secrets—secrets of forgotten kingdoms, of rivers that flowed backwards, of winters that never ended…"

The children lean closer. Even the crickets seem to pause.

The fox, they learn, made a bargain with the moon. In return for one perfect song, the moon would grant him the ability to change shape whenever he wished. But there was one rule: he could never sing that song for a human.

Years passed. The fox kept his promise… until the night a young girl with silver hair wandered to the top of the hill, lost and crying. She had run away from home after a terrible fight with her mother.

The fox watched her from the shadows. Her tears fell like tiny stars onto the grass. Something inside him stirred—something stronger than the bargain.

He stepped forward. The girl looked up, startled but not afraid.

And then, very quietly, the fox began to sing.

The moon trembled. The stars blinked in surprise. The wind stopped moving.

When the song ended, the girl was no longer crying. She smiled, wiped her eyes, and said: "Thank you. I think I understand now."

The fox said nothing. He simply bowed his head once, then turned and walked back into the forest.

The next morning the girl returned home. She never told anyone about the fox who sang with the voice of the night sky.

But from that night on, whenever the moon was full and bright, people in the village would sometimes hear the most beautiful, heartbreaking melody drifting down from the highest hill.

And the old storyteller always finishes the tale the same way:

"Some promises," he says, looking at each child in turn, "are stronger than magic. And some songs are worth breaking every rule in the universe to sing just once."

The children sit in silence for a long moment.

Then one small voice asks: "Will you tell us another one tomorrow?"

The old man smiles.

"Tomorrow," he promises, "and every night after that… as long as there are children who still want to listen."
